# 6C5: Owner attestation (D20, D21)

## What was built

- **Attestation records.** New record `venture-rule-attestation` (rule id, playbook, digest of the rule's words, `attestedOn` date, member, `withdrawnAt`/`withdrawnOn`). Each tick and withdrawal is also written to the decisions journal.
- **Engine methods.** `venture.rules.locks` (read) lists the business's locked rules: plain words, what the rule does not cover, sources, the digest, the date ticked and a line. Then the nine hard stops, one line each. `venture.rules.attest { ruleId, attest, digest? }` (approve, app only) records or withdraws the tick "I am responsible for this in my business." Refusals: a hard-stop id gives HARD_STOP, a changed rule gives APPROVAL_STALE, an unknown id gives RULE_NOT_FOUND, a rule of a playbook that is not wired yet gives RULE_NOT_ATTESTABLE. Ticking the same choice twice changes nothing.
- **Unlock.** `kit/attestation.ts` (pure): `ruleCleared(rule, attested)` passes a rule that needs no one, was confirmed by a professional, or was attested with these exact words. `kit/attestation-records.ts` reads the business's standing attestations. Wired into every client-work gate of the eight locked playbooks:
  - support desk and lead generation: `requireConfirmedRules` in delivery runs and the kit checks;
  - recruiting: `recruitPrivacyReady`;
  - job search: `requireCareerClientRule`, its order gate and agent tool;
  - newsletter: consent and export rules;
  - narration: `requireNarrationClientRule`;
  - data entry: `requireFormsClientRule`;
  - app testing: `requireAppTestingClientRule`.
  A withdrawn tick, or a tick of older words, holds the work again.
- **Hard stops.** `policy/hard-stops.ts` holds the D21 list: SEBI advice, advocates soliciting, AI diagnosis, children's data, CAPTCHA, detection evasion, LinkedIn automation, fake reviews, undisclosed AI. None of them can be attested. The advocate outreach and promotion switch in 6C6 is one enforced example, and its test shows it is still refused after every rule is ticked.
- **CA firm kept separate.** The CA firm's rules are never listed or attestable here. Its locks line points to Your CA (ICAI sign-off by the CA of record).
- **The other eight kit playbooks with locked rules** (online seller, translation, bookkeeping, podcast article, website, dev agency, IT triage, phone receptionist) are not wired yet. Their rules are refused with RULE_NOT_ATTESTABLE and their page says they still wait for a lawyer or CA (`LOCKED_ELSEWHERE`, held equal to the definitions by test). Social's 8 lawyer rules use their own legal-confirmation table and are also not wired.
- **UI.** `#/business/rules` "Your rules" (`places/business/YourRules.tsx`) gives each locked rule its own tile with a tick. "Never allowed" lists the hard stops with no tick. The page joins the business menu only while it is open, so other pages' word budgets are unchanged.
- **Fake engine.** `fake-rules-attest.ts` uses the engine's own registers and digest. The fake's gates for the eight playbooks read the same attestations.

## Deviations

- The digest is a small FNV hash of the rule's words, so the fake computes the same value. It is a staleness check, not a secret.
- The attestation screen lists only the eight wired playbooks' rules, so it never offers a tick that would not unlock anything.
- To keep the app's typecheck from pulling in the engine's contracts, `DatedRule` and `RuleRead` moved from `kit/define.ts` to a new import-free `kit/rule-type.ts`. `define.ts` re-exports both, so no other import changed.
- The support, lead-generation and recruiting rule registers now import the type from `rule-type.ts`. `kit/attestation.ts`, `core/launch-stage.ts`, `core/compliance-data.ts` and `policy/hard-stops.ts` import no engine contracts.
- `data-entry-desk/rules.ts` now checks its one rule directly. The words are the same as before, and it no longer imports `kit/rules.ts`.
